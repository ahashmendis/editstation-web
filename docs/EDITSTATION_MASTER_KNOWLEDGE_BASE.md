## 1. Executive Summary

EDITSTATION is an internal freelance video-editing operations platform
operated under Lenscape Studios. Lenscape Studios acquires client work;
EDITSTATION manages a vetted network of freelance video editors in Sri
Lanka and the operational workflow required to deliver that work.

The initial product is a private, controlled editor network---not a
public freelance marketplace, not an open bidding platform, and not a
client-facing portal. Lenscape retains the client relationship and
manages project creation, editor approval, rates, assignment, quality
control, revisions, and payment approvals.

The goal is to build a reliable system from the beginning, while keeping
the first release focused. Start with approximately 5--10 vetted
editors, pilot the workflow on real Lenscape projects, measure quality
and margins, and expand only when the operation is stable.

**Core principle:** EDITSTATION should make it easier for Lenscape to
deliver more editing work without requiring the owner to personally edit
every project.

## 3. Business Model and Operating Structure

### 3.1 How the business works

1.  Lenscape receives a video-editing request from a client.
2.  Admin creates an internal EDITSTATION project.
3.  Admin prepares the brief, assets, references, deadline,
    deliverables, and proposed editor compensation.
4.  Admin selects an approved editor or invites a specific editor.
5.  The editor reviews the opportunity and accepts, declines, or
    proposes a different rate.
6.  Admin and editor agree on the final rate and admin confirms the
    assignment.
7.  Editor completes the work and submits a review version.
8.  Admin reviews quality and either approves or requests revisions.
9.  Once approved, the project is completed.
10. Admin records and approves the editor's payment. Initial payments
    are made manually by bank transfer in Sri Lanka.

### 3.2 Responsibilities

**Lenscape / Admin** - Acquire and manage clients. - Create projects and
define deliverables. - Set initial editor compensation. - Approve
editors and control access. - Invite, assign, or reassign editors. -
Review counteroffers and confirm final compensation. - Manage deadlines,
assets, briefs, references, and project changes. - Perform quality
control. - Track revision rounds and additional paid work. - Approve and
record editor payments. - Maintain operational records and performance
metrics.

**Freelance editor** - Apply and provide profile and portfolio
information. - Complete vetting and any practical assessment. - Access
only projects made available or assigned to them. - Review the brief,
references, assets, compensation, and deadline. - Ask questions or
submit a counteroffer before assignment. - Confirm understanding of the
brief. - Edit and submit deliverables. - Respond to revision requests. -
Track completed work and payment status.

### 3.3 Initial operating constraints

-   Start with 5--10 approved editors.
-   One primary editor per project in the initial release.
-   Admin approval is required before an editor is formally assigned.
-   No public project listing or open bidding.
-   No client login or client portal in the MVP.
-   No automated payment gateway in the MVP.
-   Manual bank transfers and admin-maintained payment records.
-   Do not expose client-sensitive details unnecessarily to editors.

### 3.4 Revenue and cost accounting

Client revenue belongs to Lenscape's client engagement. Editor
compensation is recorded as a direct project cost. Do not count the same
client revenue once under Lenscape and again under EDITSTATION.

Illustrative example only: - Client pays Lenscape: LKR 15,000. - Editor
compensation: LKR 6,000. - Gross contribution before other costs and
taxes: LKR 9,000 (60% of client revenue).

This is an example to explain unit economics, not a recommended fixed
price or forecast. Track actual costs, payment fees, storage, software,
revisions, admin time, taxes, and any other project expenses.

## 4. Service Categories and Project Brief Types

The product must support three project brief types from the first
release. Do not assume every project will have a fully prescriptive
brief.

### 4.1 Fully instructed editing

Lenscape supplies most or all of the creative and technical direction: -
Source footage. - Script or sequence plan, if applicable. - Detailed
editing instructions. - Music and sound assets, if supplied. - Graphics,
logos, fonts, and brand guidelines. - Export specifications and
deliverables. - Deadline and compensation.

The editor's job is to execute the brief accurately and raise questions
about missing or contradictory instructions.

### 4.2 Reference-based editing

Lenscape supplies footage and one or more references that communicate
the intended style or outcome. The editor interprets the references and
recreates the relevant approach using the supplied project footage and
authorised assets.

A project may include multiple references. Each reference should
support: - Reference title. - URL (for example, YouTube, Vimeo, or an
authorised Drive link). - Optional uploaded reference file or excerpt,
where permitted. - Start and end timecodes or a specific timestamp. -
Description of what the editor should observe. - Priority: essential or
optional. - Category, such as pacing, transitions, colour, typography,
storytelling, framing, sound design, or motion graphics. - Access
restrictions and permission notes.

For complex reference-led work, Lenscape may require the editor to
submit a short interpretation before editing starts. The interpretation
should state what style elements they intend to reproduce, what they
will adapt, and any questions or limitations.

### 4.3 Creative-led editing

Lenscape supplies footage, the desired outcome, audience/context, brand
guidelines, and broad creative direction. The editor has greater freedom
to propose the narrative, pacing, structure, and visual treatment.

The brief should state: - Desired outcome and audience. - Core message
or story. - Brand and visual constraints. - Mandatory content or
shots. - Prohibited content or treatments. - Deliverables and technical
specifications. - Deadline and compensation.

### 4.4 Brief type and compensation

Brief type may affect complexity, but must not automatically determine
the editor's rate. Admin sets the proposed rate based on workload,
skill, turnaround, complexity, and client budget. Editors may negotiate.
The final rate must be explicitly agreed and approved before assignment.

## 5. Project Brief and Reference Workflow

Recommended workflow:

1.  Admin creates the project and selects the brief type.
2.  Admin adds deliverables, source footage, instructions, and
    references.
3.  Admin sets proposed compensation and deadline.
4.  Admin invites an editor or publishes the project only to eligible
    approved editors.
5.  Editor reviews all information and may ask questions or
    counteroffer.
6.  For complex reference-based or creative-led jobs, editor submits an
    interpretation.
7.  Editor confirms the brief, deliverables, deadline, and agreed
    compensation.
8.  Admin confirms the assignment.
9.  Editing begins.
10. Editor submits the work.
11. Admin reviews against the agreed brief and references.
12. Admin approves or requests a revision.
13. Project is completed and payment is recorded.

### 5.1 Project Brief Confirmation

Before work starts, provide a clear confirmation action. Store: - Editor
ID. - Project ID. - Confirmation timestamp. - Confirmed brief version. -
Confirmed deadline. - Confirmed deliverables. - Confirmed
compensation. - Confirmation status.

If the brief changes materially after confirmation, increment the brief
version and require re-acknowledgement where appropriate.

### 5.2 Copyright and reference handling

References communicate style and direction. They do not grant permission
to copy third-party footage, music, graphics, or other protected assets.
Editors must use project-supplied or properly licensed assets and must
not download or reuse copyrighted material without authorisation.

## 6. Revisions, Scope Changes, and Quality Control

### 6.1 Revision policy

-   Include 3 free client revision rounds by default.
-   Additional client revision rounds may be charged.
-   An editor-caused error or failure to follow the approved brief must
    be corrected without consuming the client's revision allowance.
-   A new direction, new deliverable, materially changed brief, or added
    scope is not automatically a revision; treat it as a scope change or
    a new paid task.
-   Admin controls whether a request is a revision, correction, or scope
    change.

The system must record the reason for each requested change and whether
it consumes a client revision round.

### 6.2 Review and submission

A submission should record: - Project and editor. - Submission
version. - Submission timestamp. - Preview/deliverable link or file
reference. - Editor notes. - Export details, if relevant. - Review
status. - Admin feedback.

Admin review outcomes: - Approved. - Revision requested. - Rejected /
needs discussion. - Scope change required.

Keep a history of submissions and feedback rather than overwriting
previous versions.

## 7. Editor Recruitment and Vetting

### 7.1 Initial recruitment target

Recruit 5--10 editors for the first cohort. This is a controlled
starting group, not a public marketplace.

Indicative capability mix: - 4 short-form/social editors. - 3 long-form
editors. - 2 cinematic/storytelling editors. - 1 motion-graphics
specialist.

The mix is a planning suggestion and can overlap; an editor may have
several specialisations.

### 7.2 Recruitment channels

Potential channels: - Sri Lankan freelance and creative communities. -
University media societies and relevant student communities. -
LinkedIn. - Instagram and creator communities. - Referrals from trusted
editors and production contacts.

### 7.3 Application information

Collect only what is operationally necessary: - Full name. - Email and
telephone. - General location in Sri Lanka. - Experience level. -
Editing software (DaVinci Resolve, Adobe Premiere Pro, Final Cut Pro,
CapCut, etc.). - Portfolio links. - Specialisations. - Availability and
typical turnaround. - Optional hardware/workstation information. -
Agreement to relevant terms and privacy notice.

Collect bank/payment details only when needed and protect them with
strict access controls. Avoid collecting identity documents or sensitive
data unless there is a documented legal or operational reason.

### 7.4 Vetting process

1.  Application received.
2.  Portfolio reviewed.
3.  Shortlisted.
4.  Optional practical test or interview.
5.  Test reviewed for quality, communication, and reliability.
6.  Editor approved or declined.
7.  Approved editor receives an initial level/specialisation.
8.  Editor is eligible for suitable projects.

If a practical test creates commercially useful work, consider paying
for it. Do not use substantial unpaid production tasks as a default
screening method.

Possible initial levels: - Junior. - Professional. - Specialist.

Levels should guide assignment suitability, not become an unnecessarily
complex ranking system.

## 8. Rates, Negotiation, and Payments

### 8.1 Compensation agreement

-   Admin sets a proposed rate.
-   Editor can accept, decline, or submit a counteroffer.
-   Admin can accept, reject, or propose another amount.
-   Final compensation is stored as an agreed amount.
-   Admin must approve the final rate before assignment.
-   Invitations to a specific editor should use the same agreement
    process.

Do not allow an editor to begin work while compensation is unresolved.

### 8.2 Payment workflow

Initial payments are manual bank transfers in Sri Lanka.

Payment record fields may include: - Project ID. - Editor ID. - Agreed
amount. - Currency. - Payment status. - Payment due/target date, if
used. - Transfer date. - Transaction reference. - Admin who
approved/recorded payment. - Internal notes.

Suggested payment statuses: - Not due. - Pending approval. - Approved
for payment. - Processing. - Paid. - On hold / disputed.

Only authorised admin users may mark a payment as paid. Store only
necessary payment details; never store online banking passwords, PINs,
or authentication codes.

## 11. Technical Architecture

### 11.1 Recommended initial stack

-   Frontend/full-stack framework: Next.js with TypeScript.
-   UI: Tailwind CSS and shadcn/ui.
-   Database: Supabase PostgreSQL.
-   Authentication: Supabase Auth.
-   Authorisation: PostgreSQL Row Level Security (RLS).
-   Hosting: Vercel.
-   Email notifications: Resend or another suitable transactional email
    provider.
-   Source control: GitHub.
-   Development assistant: Claude Code.
-   Initial asset storage: restricted Google Drive or Dropbox links.
-   Later asset storage: Cloudflare R2 or another S3-compatible object
    store.

Treat this as the current recommendation, not an irreversible choice.
Verify current vendor pricing, limits, terms, and regional suitability
before production deployment.

### 11.2 File and media storage

Video footage is large. Do not store large video files inside PostgreSQL
or pass large media through ordinary application-server routes.

**MVP:** use Google Drive/Dropbox or another existing storage provider
with carefully controlled permissions. The application stores metadata
and authorised links. Editors should receive access only to project
assets they are permitted to view.

**Later:** consider Cloudflare R2 with S3-compatible access,
direct-to-storage uploads, signed/time-limited URLs, upload limits,
retention rules, and lifecycle/archival policies.

Do not expose permanent public links to confidential footage. Validate
access and permissions when an editor is assigned, removed, or a project
is closed.

### 11.3 Security architecture

-   Supabase Auth for identity.
-   RLS policies for all user-sensitive tables.
-   Editors can see/update only their own profile and permitted records.
-   Editors can see only projects explicitly made available to them or
    assigned to them.
-   Editors must not see another editor's compensation, bank details, or
    private profile data.
-   Admin-only operations must be enforced server-side and at the
    database layer.
-   Validate all user input on the server.
-   Use least-privilege service credentials; never expose service-role
    secrets to the browser.
-   Keep secrets in environment variables.
-   Maintain activity/audit logs for sensitive actions.
-   Use secure, restricted asset links.
-   Implement backups and a tested recovery process.
-   Add rate limits and sensible authentication protections.
-   Do not rely on hiding UI controls as authorisation.

### 11.4 Suggested database entities

Initial tables: - `users` or Supabase Auth user references. -
`editor_profiles`. - `editor_skills`. - `projects`. -
`project_assets`. - `project_references`. - `assignments`. -
`negotiations`. - `brief_confirmations`. - `editor_interpretations`. -
`submissions`. - `revisions`. - `payments`. - `activity_logs`.

Design relationally. Do not put important, queryable reference
information into an opaque JSON array. A `project_references` table
should link each reference to a project and hold its title, URL/file
reference, timecodes, notes, priority, category, and permission/access
metadata.

Use foreign keys, indexes, created/updated timestamps, constraints, and
soft-delete/archive conventions where appropriate. Document the schema
and migration strategy.

## 12. Privacy, Legal, and Business Administration

This section is a checklist for professional advice, not legal or tax
advice.

### 12.1 Business structure

Confirm Lenscape Studios' current legal status before deciding whether
EDITSTATION needs any separate registration. Consider the appropriate
sole-proprietor or company structure with a Sri Lankan
accountant/lawyer. If registering a company, check current Registrar of
Companies/eROC requirements.

### 12.2 Contracts and editor terms

Have a Sri Lankan lawyer review an editor agreement covering: - Scope
and deliverables. - Compensation and payment timing. - Deadlines and
late delivery. - Revision policy. - Scope changes and extra charges. -
Confidentiality and client information. - Ownership/assignment/licensing
of deliverables. - Third-party assets, fonts, music, plugins, and stock
content. - Portfolio/showreel permission. - Data and footage security. -
Cancellation and non-performance. - Disputes and applicable law.

Do not assume copyright ownership transfers automatically. The contract
should explicitly address assignment or licensing of economic rights,
permitted uses, and any relevant moral-rights considerations.

### 12.3 Privacy and data protection

Sri Lanka's Personal Data Protection Act No. 9 of 2022 has been amended,
including by Act No. 22 of 2025. Confirm the currently applicable
provisions, commencement dates, regulations, and compliance obligations
with qualified local counsel.

Plan for: - Clear privacy notice. - Purpose limitation and data
minimisation. - Access controls. - Retention and deletion rules. -
Secure handling of contact, portfolio, and payment data. - A process for
data access/correction/deletion requests where applicable. - Incident
response. - Vendor review for hosting, email, and storage providers.

### 12.4 Tax and accounting

Confirm current Sri Lankan tax registration, income tax, VAT,
withholding, invoicing, and recordkeeping obligations with a qualified
accountant or the Inland Revenue Department. Do not rely on historic
threshold or rate figures without checking the rules in force when the
business starts trading.

## 13. Rollout Plan

A four-week plan is an ambitious working target, not a delivery
guarantee.

-   Confirm Lenscape's legal/business status.
-   Conduct name/domain/trademark checks.
-   Define service categories and pricing logic.
-   Draft editor terms and privacy notice for legal review.

## 14. Pilot Metrics and Decision Gates

Suggested internal targets for the initial pilot---not industry
benchmarks: - 5--10 approved editors. - 10--20 real assignments
processed through the workflow. - At least 90% on-time delivery. - At
least 70% first-submission approval. - Editor abandonment below 5%. -
Accurate payment records for every completed assignment. - Positive
contribution on each service category after direct project costs.

Track: - Time from project creation to assignment. - Editor acceptance
and counteroffer rate. - Actual turnaround time. - On-time delivery. -
First-submission approval. - Average revision rounds. - Editor-caused
corrections. - Actual editor cost per project. - Gross contribution per
project. - Editor availability and retention. - Admin time spent per
project.

Do not scale the editor pool simply because more people apply. Scale
when quality, reliability, and positive project contribution are
repeatable.

## 16. Product Acceptance Criteria

The MVP is not ready for real client work until all of the following are
true:

### Authentication and permissions

-   Admin and editor roles are enforced server-side.
-   Unapproved editors cannot access restricted projects.
-   Editors cannot access other editors' private records or payment
    data.
-   Admin-only actions cannot be invoked by editor accounts.
